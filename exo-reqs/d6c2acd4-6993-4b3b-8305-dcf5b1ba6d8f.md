## Job story

When a spec's `beforeAll` (or a test body) overflows its budget while an Obsidian launch is still in flight and Playwright runs `afterAll` → `close()` / the spec's abort, I want the launch that nobody is waiting for to END right there — the in-flight CDP-port wait rejected on the abandon itself, no CDP connect to whatever comes up on :9222 afterwards, and no fresh launcher started by the retry helper — so that the abandoned attempt can neither keep polling :9222 for 45 s nor attach to the NEXT spec's Obsidian, and the helper cannot go on spawning up to 8 Electron processes that no one will ever close.

## Context

- Source: ticket `4abffc07-0d99-4ad5-8a71-ec5e6b4ecdab` (LOW-1 + LOW-2 of the ORCH review of PR kitelev/exocortex#4252; parent techbacklog `bbac67ce`). Sibling of req `dad111a2-d4b9-42e2-a1c7-2a9c0ea702ae` (launch() does not start the NEXT attempt after its caller abandoned it) and of req `675d37a6-054a-4acb-873b-cba9e90c6d3e` (`waitForPluginReady` reports a concurrent close() explicitly). Neither governs what the IN-FLIGHT attempt does between the abandon and its own failure, nor the helper that wraps `launch()` in an 8-attempt loop with a fresh launcher per attempt — that is this requirement.
- Mechanism (`packages/obsidian-plugin/tests/e2e/utils/obsidian-launcher.ts` @ `6c89b6b3`): `close()` (`:747-757`) sets `abandoned` while a launch is in flight and SIGKILLs Electron, but `launchAttempt` (`:103-252`) reads the flag nowhere: `waitForPort(this.cdpPort, 45000)` (`:162`, `retryCheck` `:565` looks only at `Date.now() - startTime > timeout`) keeps polling `/json/version` on :9222 every 500 ms, and `chromium.connectOverCDP` (`:166`) follows unconditionally. Reproduced on `6c89b6b3` with fake timers: after `close()` the attempt stays pending with a poll timer armed and rejects 45 s later with "Timeout waiting for port", having issued 89 more requests — and if another spec brings its own Obsidian up on :9222 inside that window, the orphan resolves against the foreign process and connects to it.
- Mechanism (helpers): `launchObsidianWithPlugin` existed as TWO copies — `tests/e2e/eka-gui/eka-gui-helpers.ts` (`:350-374`, used by 6 eka-gui specs) and a local one in `tests/e2e/eka/eka-obsidian-leg.spec.ts` (`:396-420`, 3 call sites) — each looping up to `MAX_LAUNCH_ATTEMPTS = 8` with `new ObsidianLauncher(vaultPath)` per attempt. A spec's `launcher` variable is `null` until the helper returns, so its `afterAll` (`if (launcher) await launcher.close()`) has nothing to close while the helper is inside an attempt; reproduced on `6c89b6b3`: after the simulated `afterAll` and one failed attempt, a second launcher exists that nobody holds.
- Design (plan-gate `4abffc07`, ORCH `main-35699` 2026-09-17 plan-ok, class 2): (a) the wait that is blocking an attempt registers a one-slot cancel callback on the launcher; `close()` while a launch is in flight fires it (before the teardown) so `waitForPort` rejects on the abandon itself and disarms its poll timer; `launchAttempt` additionally refuses to `connectOverCDP` once `abandoned` is set (defence in depth for the window between the port coming up and the connect). (b) ONE shared helper `tests/e2e/utils/launch-obsidian-with-plugin.ts` with signature `launchObsidianWithPlugin(vaultPath, label, { signal?: AbortSignal, log? })`: on `abort` the helper closes the in-flight launcher (which cancels its wait per (a) and stops its retries per `dad111a2`) and starts no further attempt; without a signal the behaviour is byte-identical to before. `eka-gui-helpers.ts` keeps a thin wrapper (same signature, `[eka-gui]` log prefix), `eka-obsidian-leg.spec.ts` imports the shared helper; all 7 consumer specs wire an `AbortController` in `describe` scope, pass `{ signal }`, and call `abort()` as the first line of `afterAll`.

## Statement (Gherkin)

```gherkin
Feature: ObsidianLauncher abandon cancels the in-flight attempt, and launchObsidianWithPlugin is one abandonable helper

  Background:
    Given launchAttempt spawns Obsidian, then awaits waitForPort(9222, 45000), then chromium.connectOverCDP
    And close() on a launcher whose launch() is in flight marks the launch abandoned (req dad111a2)
    And launchObsidianWithPlugin retries the whole launch up to 8 times with a fresh ObsidianLauncher per attempt

  Scenario: close() during the CDP-port wait ends the attempt on the abandon itself
    Given launchAttempt 1 is inside waitForPort and the port keeps refusing connections
    When the caller invokes close()
    Then the attempt rejects with an error naming the abandonment ("waiting for port 9222 was abandoned by its caller") without waiting for the next poll or the 45 s ceiling
    And no poll timer is left armed and no further /json/version request is issued
    And chromium.connectOverCDP is not called

  Scenario: without a close() the CDP-port wait is unchanged (control)
    Given launchAttempt 1 is inside waitForPort and the port keeps refusing connections
    When nobody calls close()
    Then the wait keeps polling every 500 ms and rejects with "Timeout waiting for port 9222 after 45000ms" once the ceiling passes

  Scenario: a port that comes up still proceeds to the CDP connect (control)
    Given launchAttempt 1 is inside waitForPort and nobody calls close()
    When /json/version answers 200
    Then chromium.connectOverCDP("http://localhost:9222", { timeout: 30000 }) is called exactly once

  Scenario: an attempt abandoned between the port coming up and the CDP connect does not connect
    Given waitForPort resolved and the caller invoked close() before launchAttempt continued
    When launchAttempt reaches the CDP connect
    Then it rejects with an error naming the abandonment ("not connecting over CDP")
    And chromium.connectOverCDP is not called

  Scenario: abort during an attempt closes the in-flight launcher and starts no further attempt
    Given launchObsidianWithPlugin was called with an AbortSignal and attempt 1's launch() is in flight
    When the signal is aborted
    Then close() is invoked on that in-flight launcher exactly once, immediately
    And after that launch() fails, launchObsidianWithPlugin rejects with an error naming the abandonment ("launch abandoned by its caller") and does not construct a second ObsidianLauncher

  Scenario: abort during the plugin-load wait behaves the same
    Given attempt 1's launch() returned and the helper is waiting for the plugin to load
    When the signal is aborted
    Then close() is invoked on that launcher and launchObsidianWithPlugin rejects with the abandonment error without constructing a second ObsidianLauncher

  Scenario: without a signal the retry loop is unchanged (control)
    Given launchObsidianWithPlugin was called without an AbortSignal
    When attempt 1 fails with an ordinary error and attempt 2 succeeds
    Then the helper closes launcher 1, constructs launcher 2 and returns it

  Scenario: an already-aborted signal starts nothing (control)
    Given the AbortSignal is already aborted
    When launchObsidianWithPlugin is called with it
    Then it rejects with the abandonment error and constructs no ObsidianLauncher

  Scenario: after a successful return the helper no longer reacts to the signal
    Given launchObsidianWithPlugin returned a launcher
    When the signal is aborted later (the spec's afterAll)
    Then the helper does not call close() on the returned launcher (the spec owns it)

  Scenario: exactly one helper implementation exists
    Given the e2e suite
    Then neither tests/e2e/eka-gui/eka-gui-helpers.ts nor tests/e2e/eka/eka-obsidian-leg.spec.ts defines its own launchObsidianWithPlugin retry loop, tryLoadPlugin or MAX_LAUNCH_ATTEMPTS — both use tests/e2e/utils/launch-obsidian-with-plugin.ts
```

## Non-goals

- Not changing `waitForPluginReady`, the trust-dialog probe/click, `waitForVaultReady`, the window wait after the CDP connect, the shard assignments, the Playwright configs or CI workflows.
- Not changing the launcher's retry count (3) / backoff, nor the helper's attempt budget (8) / per-attempt plugin-load ceiling (60 s).
- Not making `close()` interrupt a `chromium.connectOverCDP` that is already in progress: the teardown kills Electron, the connect fails on its own, and `launch()` then does not retry (req `dad111a2`).
- Not changing test bodies of the 7 consumer specs beyond the AbortController wiring (controller in `describe` scope, `{ signal }` argument, `abort()` first in `afterAll`).
